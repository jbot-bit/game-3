# Hands Full

A physics party game for up to three players on Windows LAN. Plain, faceless people, a circular Knockout survival arena, padded-stick Gladiator fights, a Wipeout balance race, twenty co-op courses and earned cosmetic surprise bags.

## Play together

1. Download `HandsFull.exe` from https://github.com/jbot-bit/game-3/releases/tag/hands-full-v0.8.0 on every PC and double-click it. It is one self-contained file. The ZIP is an optional copy with the same executable and printed guide.
2. Enter a name. One player selects **Knockout**, **Gladiator**, **Wipeout** or **Co-op** and chooses **Host game**. Press **Esc** to see and copy the host's join address. Co-op offers **20 numbered levels**.
3. The other players enter that address and choose **Join**. Use the same home network and the same build.
4. Play your chosen mode, then open the earned surprise bag. The host can replay. Leave to the main menu to host a different mode.

No editor, installation, account or internet service is required. Credits and complete third-party notices are built into the opening menu. If Windows asks, allow the game on your private network. UDP port: 24573. Guest Wi-Fi isolation can prevent joining; port forwarding is unnecessary.

## Knockout, Gladiator and Wipeout

**Knockout:** stay on one circular pad while two padded sweepers move around it. Jump the long low bar and keep clear of the shorter high arm. Left click swings your stick to knock opponents off; hold right click to brace against their attacks. Every 15 seconds the sweepers accelerate, reaching 2.5 times their starting speed after one minute. Warning beats announce each escalation. At 30 seconds the pad begins shrinking from 9 metres to 6 metres in radius over the next 30 seconds, forcing opponents into closer range. Last player standing wins the round; first to three round wins takes the match. Hazards wait through the countdown and reset for each round. Two or three players compete; solo is practice. The same draw, disconnect and late-join rules as Gladiator apply.

**Gladiator:** two or three contestants on a raised foam arena. **Left mouse swings your padded stick; right mouse braces** against hits while slowing your movement. Aim with the mouse, move with WASD and jump with Space. Push everyone else off to win a round; first to **three round wins** takes the match. F forfeits your round. Simultaneous falls and 90-second timeouts draw. Late arrivals wait for the next round; a disconnect cancels the current round without awarding a point. Solo is practice without rewards. Eliminated players spectate until the next countdown.

**Wipeout:** one to three runners cross two tipping beams, rotating padded sweepers and four jump podiums. Reach the **three checkpoints in order**, then the finish. WASD/mouse move and look; Space jumps; F returns to your checkpoint. Falls and recovery keep the clock running. First finisher wins, while the others can finish until the five-minute limit. Leaving an unfinished race forfeits that connection's result; reconnecting starts at checkpoint zero. A run with at least one finisher earns a bag when everyone finishes or time expires.

All three competitive modes use equal movement, with no powers, hand grabs or grapples. Collected paints and accessories carry across all modes; a saved Knockout/Gladiator match or Wipeout run earns one shared three-card bag.

## Co-op controls

| Input | Action |
|---|---|
| WASD / mouse | Move / look |
| Hold left or right mouse | Reach and grab with that hand; release to let go |
| Hold Q / release Q | Fire the grapple where you aim / detach |
| G | Switch grapple mode: Pull or Swing, including while attached |
| Space | Jump |
| Hold R | Go floppy; release to stand up |
| E near the fan lever | Reverse the wind for everyone |
| Hold E on a control pad | Operate a team bridge or lift |
| E at the far latch | Lock a completed team obstacle open |
| F | Recover at your checkpoint |
| Esc | Menu and host join address |
| M / F3 | Mute / FPS display |
| 1 / 2 / 3 | Choose a random power |

Look toward the thing you want to grab. Look up to lift it. Release a hand if it catches a support. Falling returns you to your checkpoint; loose props also recover if they fall away.

Movement accelerates and changes direction quickly. Release WASD to brake on the ground; airborne jumps and grapple launches keep their momentum. Swift Step still gives a speed boost.

Aim at fixed scenery or the mint-and-coral overhead grapple beams. The center reticle lights up when a surface is in range. **Pull** reels you toward the attachment point. **Swing** keeps a fixed rope length so you can build an arc and carry your momentum; release Q to launch out of the swing. Press G while attached to change between reeling and swinging. Everyone has both modes immediately, alongside their chosen power and both hand grabs.

The grapple reaches 28 metres and attaches to solid, fixed scenery. It cannot attach to teammates or movable props. If a shot misses, release Q and try again. The rope runs straight to its anchor; it does not wrap around obstacles.

**First Crossing approach:** cross the loose bridges, then balance the seesaw and jump to the team staging deck.

**Counterweight:** put blocks on the low end of the tipping board, or have a friend steady it. Blocks can stay when that friend follows. Reach the checkpoint, turn right and cross the loose planks. The fan lever affects the whole crew.

**Levels 3–20:** Dogleg Docks, Stepping Stones, High Walk, Cargo Maze, Twin Bridges, Switchback, Crosswind, Rooftop Hop, Balance Beam, Freight Spiral, Split Decision, Zigzag, Crane Yard, Windy Steps, Narrow Escape, Round Trip, Skybridge and Final Shift. Each has its own approach route and checkpoint. Use the overhead grapple beams for shortcuts or recovery.

**Every level continues into three mandatory team obstacles:** two people hold E on separate colored pads while the third crosses the drawbridge and locks the far latch. At the lift, the runner boards the lowered platform, rides it up with both operators helping, and locks the upper latch. At the cargo bridge, the runner grabs the marked crate, delivers it onto the far square, then locks the latch. The operators can follow after each latch; that stage becomes a shared checkpoint. You can change roles between stages. Grappling directly to extraction cannot complete the objectives.

All levels are available immediately. **Next course** takes the whole crew forward after a saved clear; level 20 returns to level 1. Your power collection and clear count carry across the full set. A checkpoint lasts for the current run; choose a level from the host menu when starting a new session.

The menu keeps the game running and releases your hands and grapple. If the bridges get stranded, the host can choose **Esc → Restart course**. This restarts for everyone; collected powers stay saved.

## Progress

Choose one of three random powers: a stronger jump, extra weight, faster movement, gentler falling or stronger arms. Finish the course and the powers your crew used become permanent **Collected powers**. On later runs, each player can choose a collected power instead of a random offer. Keep using the same PC as host: it stores the crew's shared collection and clear count.

Every saved clear also earns one **three-card surprise bag**. Flip the cards to reveal paints and optional accessories; rare and higher cards have animated foil. Each card rolls Common **60%**, Rare **28%**, Epic **10%**, or Legendary **2%**. A new item is preferred within its rolled tier; duplicates become possible when that tier is collected. These are earned cosmetics with no movement or power bonuses.

Use **Customize** in the power screen or pause menu to equip collected paint, caps, beanies, sprouts, headphones, crowns or halos. The default body stays blank and faceless; choose None to remove an accessory. The host saves the shared card collection and each crew slot's appearance. Joining another host uses that host's collection.

Only successful clears save rewards. Failed saves show **Retry save** and retain the same pending bag until saving succeeds. A successful retry keeps the reward screen open. No coins, purchases, equipment durability or escalating grind. Crew level is a record of clears; it does not make one player stronger than another.

Save: `%APPDATA%\OneMoreCast\physics_party\progress.json`. The parent folder is retained for compatibility. The previous fishing game's `household.json` is separate.

## Build scope

This family release contains twenty approach layouts, a shared three-obstacle teamwork sequence on each, five powers and fifteen collectible cosmetics. Keyboard and mouse are supported; controllers and internet matchmaking are not included. Actual three-PC responsiveness, first-time solve times and family enjoyment still need a household play session.

## Source

Open `project.godot` in Godot 4.7.2 and run `prototypes/physics_party/main.tscn`. The project's default scene belongs to the separate fishing game. Build: `powershell -ExecutionPolicy Bypass -File tools/build_hands_full.ps1 -ReleaseName hands-full-0.8.0 -RenderSmoke`.

- `main.gd`: input, camera, shared state, recovery and completion.
- `puppet.gd` / `puppet.tscn`: active physical bodies, hand joints and smooth rendering.
- `party_world.gd`: First Crossing, shared props, fan and checkpoints.
- `party_counterweight_world.gd`: Counterweight geometry.
- `party_courses.gd` / `party_level_data.gd` / `party_level_world.gd`: twenty course IDs, eighteen authored layouts and their shared builder.
- `party_session.gd`: host-authoritative LAN protocol and pose delivery.
- `party_hud.gd` / `party_rules.gd`: screens, power choices and atomic saves.
- `party_teamwork.gd`: three physical team stages, validated participant actions and shared checkpoints.
- `party_cosmetics.gd` / `party_accessories.gd`: rarity catalog, card draws and collision-free character accessories.
- `party_audio.gd`: short original sound cues generated once at startup.

The controller/model adapts PiCode9560's MIT-licensed active-ragdoll project. The adaptation adds per-player input, fixed-step control, remote presentation, recovery and LAN integration. See `vendor/picode/PROVENANCE.md`. Other researched repositories were references, not bundled frameworks. Current test evidence is in `docs/playtest/HANDS_FULL_STATUS.md` in the source project.
